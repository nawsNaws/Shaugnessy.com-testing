A personal reflection

Why I chose STEM

Representation, problem solving, community, and the experiences that led me toward engineering.

Read reflection

As a female-identifying individual interested in engineering, maths, and technology, I have long noticed the lack of representation in STEM spaces. It is upsetting to see this trend continue, despite society's many efforts to remove barriers for women in STEM. Throughout the many clubs and activities I have participated in, I was consistently surrounded by an astonishingly male-dominated populace, and it disappoints me to see the lack of diversity in the community. In an effort to fix this issue, I currently promote resources and opportunities to the younger generation of women in my community, and I sincerely hope they can discover the joys of STEM.

A recent instance of my efforts can be seen in my efforts to promote math to the new students entering my high school via social media and at school information events. During the grade 9 orientation, I volunteered to give tours of the school, ensuring I pointed out the Math Contests and special opportunities along the way to inspire my younger female peers. I have also created a math club to further mentor and inspire other students, which I promote through social media. Even in my part time job, I ensure that the pre-teen girl I have been hired to tutor develops confidence in STEM. We talk of designing cars together in the future, developing solutions to the issues she points out, or simply pursuing her interest in problem solving.

I am extremely passionate about problem solving and mathematics. I have always loved learning about the relationships of numbers in school as a kid, but my true passion began with a Waterloo math contest in grade 7. I believe since then, I've participated in almost every single math competition available to me from my school. The questions on these contests were nothing like the ones in class, and they required a whole new type of thinking that I fell in love with. Connecting basic concepts together to uncover a deeper relationship is so extremely satisfying it's almost addicting.

Through the CTMC collaborative Waterloo math contest, I met a group of friends who inspire and drive me today. Every new contest, we would have a friendly competition to see who could medal for each contest. My first medal, on the Fermat, felt amazing, but once I finally achieved my goal of coming top in the school, I realised I had been aiming too low, and I hoped to learn even more. I'm sure my friends felt the same. Our passion began to grow, leading to our research in mathematics, and eventually, I pitched the idea of a math club to the group. Together, we co-founded the club, and provided other students a space to learn while designating a time to consistently practice deep thinking and mathematics. Presently, we are bringing more opportunities to our school such as the COMC or the AMC math contests, and inspiring more students to challenge themselves.

I would love to join a post secondary engineering program, as I have a strong love for STEM and creating things. I have always known I would go into a math-based post-secondary program. However, it wasn't until I participated in a hackathon at the University of Toronto, namely UTRA Hacks, that I got exposure to working in an engineering team and decided to pursue an educational path that could allow me to explore this. At this two-day event, I worked tirelessly with a team from my school to engineer and program a robot that could complete a complex race course. Despite being one of the only high-school participants, our team managed to surpass the progress of many others. However, the experience also taught me crucial lessons and gave me respect for the engineering process.

The year after UTRA Hacks, I joined my school's robotics club and accompanied our FTC team 22664 at the Canadian Cup, an international robotics competition. I am currently leading programming in my team in the club this year, and I will be competing at the FTC events. Programming and technology has been another large part of my life, as I’ve worked on many personal projects such as a custom Iron Man Arc Reactor halloween costume to practice soldering, or personal websites to practice front end development and design. I have also applied my programming knowledge to assist my team in University of Toronto’s SLC coding contest challenge this year, and we won second place in Ontario. Technology is the gateway to creativity, and I look forward to working on new projects, and exploring my STEM career!

My future in engineering

Bringing engineering, math, and business together to build impactful innovations.

Read reflection

My primary goal is to combine engineering, math, and business to build impactful innovations. My passion sparked through hackathons, where I got early exposure to rapid prototyping and a deep respect for the engineering lifecycle. This inspired me to join my school's robotics club, where I currently lead programming in my team, managing software logic and systems integration. Attending last year’s Canada Cup FTC robotics competition gave me a firsthand look at advanced system design from various international teams, and I currently integrate these insights into my robotics work. My ideal future career reflects this, as I find overseeing projects and systems design extremely intriguing. I have consulted my mom, who works as a VP in IT, about my goals, and she has given me guidance, encouraging me to think ahead to the rapidly-changing career landscape and artificial intelligence progressions.
